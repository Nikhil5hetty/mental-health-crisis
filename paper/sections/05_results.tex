\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We first summarise the earlier study that motivated this one (Section~\ref{sec:res-motivation}) and then answer RQ1 to
RQ3.
All intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals; Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} lists every hypothesis with its recorded verdict,
and Appendix~\ref{app:tables} gives the full per-phrase tables.

\subsection{Motivation: Disagreement in Crisis Judgements}
\label{sec:res-motivation}

In an earlier pre-registered study on the same BHH messages (commit \PlanRoneDemoCommit{}), we found that crisis
judgements are often contested.
The four human annotators of BHH split on whether a message signals a crisis for a share of \RoneShareHumanSplitAllFull{}
of the \RoneNItemsAll{} messages.
When an LLM-judge panel was decoded with CWW and allowed to escalate when its verdict was contested
(\emph{escalate when unsure}), recall of the replies that the human raters judged unsafe rose by
\RtwoDIFFMThreeBVsMThreeRecallSThreeSTwoFull{} over the crisp mean of all judge runs, at a change in precision of
\RtwoDIFFMThreeBVsMThreePrecisionSThreeSTwoFull{}.
Both results point to the same question: if uncertainty is kept and put into words, whose meaning of those words does
the system use?

\subsection{RQ1: The Citizen Codebook}
\label{sec:res-rq1}

Respondents agree on the extremes and disagree in the middle.
The FOU area, which measures how much the demographic groups differ, ranges from \WoneFouAreaAlmostNoChance{} for
\word{\WoneNarrowestTerm} to \WoneFouAreaUnlikely{} for \word{\WoneWidestTerm} (Table~\ref{tab:rq1fou} in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
Two phrases are almost points for respondents: the middle half of them gave the same number for \word{About Even} (IQR
\WoneIqrAboutEven{}), and nearly so for \word{Will Happen} (IQR \WoneIqrWillHappen{}).
The level word \word{Likely}, for example, has a centroid of $[\WoneCentroidLeftLikely, \WoneCentroidRightLikely]$ and a
centre of \WoneCentreLikely{}.
All \WoneNGroups{} groups had enough answers for every phrase (\WoneGroupCellsExcluded{} exclusions), and the mean Jaccard
similarity between two groups ranged from \WoneGroupJaccardMin{} to \WoneGroupJaccardMax{}; native speakers of similar
age agreed most.

The absolute judgements are internally consistent: in \WoneConsistencyConsistentSharePct{}\% of the
\WoneConsistencyNPairs{} pairwise choices, respondents chose the phrase to which they had given the higher number
(\WoneConsistencyTieSharePct{}\% ties, \WoneConsistencyInconsistentSharePct{}\% inconsistent).
The codebook is robust: excluding the \WoneInattentionExcluded{} inattentive respondents barely changes the IT2 word
models (mean Jaccard with the primary codebook \WoneSensInattentionExcludedMeanJaccardPrimary{}).
An envelope over bootstrap resamples of all respondents is much narrower than the envelope over groups (mean FOU area
\WoneSensBootstrapMeanFouArea{} against \WoneMeanFouArea{}; mean Jaccard \WoneSensBootstrapMeanJaccardPrimary{}), so the
FOU of the citizen codebook reflects differences between groups, not sampling noise.

\subsection{RQ2: The LLM Codebook}
\label{sec:res-rq2}

LLM meanings overlap only partly with the citizen codebook (Figure~\ref{fig:jaccard}).
The Jaccard similarity of the two IT2 sets is highest for \word{Chances are Slight}
(\WtwoJaccardItTwoChancesAreSlightLlmItTwoFull{}) and lowest for \word{Better than Even}
(\WtwoJaccardItTwoBetterThanEvenLlmItTwoFull{}) and \word{About Even} (\WtwoJaccardItTwoAboutEvenLlmItTwoFull{}).

\paragraph{H2a is reversed.}
We expected less overlap for hedge words than for extreme words.
The opposite holds: the mean Jaccard is \WtwoMeanJaccardHedgeHedgeLlmItTwoFull{} for hedge words and
\WtwoMeanJaccardExtremeExtremeLlmItTwoFull{} for extreme words, so $D = \WtwoHTwoADHedgeMinusExtremeLlmItTwoFull$ lies
entirely above zero, and H2a is not supported.
The persona prompt P2 gives the same direction ($D = \WtwoPtwoHTwoADHedgeMinusExtremeLlmItTwoFull$).

\paragraph{The largest shifts.}
The centres of three phrases move by more than twenty percentage points.
\word{Will Happen} moves down by \WtwoCentroidShiftItTwoWillHappenLlmItTwoFull{} points: respondents treat it as near
certainty, whereas the models disagree; one always answers $100$, one places it around three quarters, and one spreads
its answers over most of the scale, and the LLM envelope contains all three.
The negative phrases \word{Improbable} (\WtwoCentroidShiftItTwoImprobableLlmItTwoFull{}) and \word{Highly Unlikely}
(\WtwoCentroidShiftItTwoHighlyUnlikelyLlmItTwoFull{}) move up, so that the LLMs read them as less unlikely than
respondents do.
Among the level words of the decision rule, \word{Likely} moves down by \WtwoCentroidShiftItTwoLikelyLlmItTwoFull{} points.

\paragraph{H2b: narrower, but not for every model.}
Three models use most phrases more narrowly than respondents: the share of phrases with a smaller IQR is
\WtwoHTwoBShareAllGemmaTwoNineBFull{} for \WModelGemmaTwoNineB{}, \WtwoHTwoBShareAllMistralSevenBFull{} for
\WModelMistralSevenB{} and \WtwoHTwoBShareAllQwenTwoFiveSevenBFull{} for \WModelQwenTwoFiveSevenB{}.
For \WModelLlamaThreeOneEightB{} (\WtwoHTwoBShareAllLlamaThreeOneEightBFull{}) and for the answers of all models pooled
(\WtwoHTwoBShareAllPooledLlmFull{}) H2b is not supported, because the models disagree with each other even where each is
narrow.
Under P2, \WModelLlamaThreeOneEightB{} is never narrower (\WtwoPtwoHTwoBShareAllLlamaThreeOneEightBFull{}).

\paragraph{Collapsed meanings.}
In \WtwoCollapsesPOne{} of \WtwoCellsPOne{} model--phrase cells under P1 (\WtwoCollapsesPTwo{} under P2), a model gave the
same number in at least nine of ten answers at temperature \WTemperature{}, so its word model collapsed to a point:
\WtwoCollapsesPOneMistralSevenB{} cells for \WModelMistralSevenB{}, \WtwoCollapsesPOneQwenTwoFiveSevenB{} for
\WModelQwenTwoFiveSevenB{}, \WtwoCollapsesPOneGemmaTwoNineB{} for \WModelGemmaTwoNineB{} and
\WtwoCollapsesPOneLlamaThreeOneEightB{} for \WModelLlamaThreeOneEightB{}.
Typical collapsed answers are round numbers, such as fifty for \word{About Even} or \word{Probable}.
\WModelMistralSevenB{} gave too few valid answers for \word{Will Happen} and was excluded for that phrase; the width ratio
for \word{Will Happen} is extreme (\WtwoWidthRatioWillHappenPooledLlm{} for the pooled answers) because the respondent IQR
is only \WtwoCitizenIqrWillHappenCitizen{}.

\subsection{RQ3: Does the Mismatch Change the Decision?}
\label{sec:res-rq3}

With the LLM codebook instead of the citizen codebook, the crisis word changes for a share of
\WthreeWordDisagreementRateLlmVsCitizenFull{} of the \WthreeNItems{} messages, and the action changes for
\WthreeActionFlipRateLlmVsCitizenFull{}, that is \WthreeNFlips{} messages.
The lower bound of the interval is above zero, so H3 is supported.
The flips lean towards under-escalation: in \WthreeNUnder{} messages the LLM codebook leads to the less urgent action
(\WthreeUnderEscalationRateLlmVsCitizenFull{}) and in \WthreeNOver{} to the more urgent one
(\WthreeOverEscalationRateLlmVsCitizenFull{}).
All flips lie between A0 and A1: \WthreeMatrixLlmVsCitizenAOneAZero{} messages move from suggesting a helpline to simply
continuing, and \WthreeMatrixLlmVsCitizenAZeroAOne{} the other way.
No message moves to or from a human handover.
Against the six group codebooks, the flip rate ranges up to \WthreeActionFlipRateLlmVsNativeThreeFiveFiveFourFull{}
(native speakers aged 35--54; Table~\ref{tab:rq3rates} in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\paragraph{The role of the fallback.}
The fallback for crisis degrees that no word covers decides many verbalisations (\WthreeNFallbackCitizen{} messages with
the citizen codebook, \WthreeNFallbackLlm{} with the LLM codebook).
Most of these are harmless: \WthreeNZeroOrHundred{} messages have $m = 0$ or $m = 100$, which lie just outside the
quantile trapezoids of \word{Remote Chance} and \word{Almost Certain} in every codebook, and all codebooks agree on them.
The fallback matters at intermediate degrees.
The LLM codebook covers no level word between \WthreeLlmGapLow{} and \WthreeLlmGapHigh{} (Figure~\ref{fig:levels}), so
the \WthreeNGapThreeThree{} messages at a crisis degree of one third fall back to \word{Remote Chance}, whereas respondents
call that degree a \word{Realistic Possibility}.
Of the \WthreeNFlips{} flips, \WthreeNFlipsFallback{} arise in this way and \WthreeNFlipsClean{} have both words chosen by
maximum membership.
Two sensitivity analyses, added after the result was known and therefore post hoc, show that the H3 conclusion does not depend on the fallback: counting only messages decided without a fallback, the flip rate is
\WthreePosthocANoFallbackItemsOnlyActionFlipRateLlmVsCitizenFull{}, and with a fallback that picks the more severe
neighbouring word it is \WthreePosthocBSevereNeighbourFallbackActionFlipRateLlmVsCitizenFull{}.
